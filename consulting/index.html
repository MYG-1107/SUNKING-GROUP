Good advice starts with good questions

Organisations often know that something needs to improve but are not yet sure where to begin. It may be a management process that no longer works, a people issue that affects performance, a change that needs better communication or a learning programme that needs a clearer purpose.

Our first step is to understand the context: what is happening, who is affected, what has already been tried and what a useful outcome would look like. Only then can the scope, deliverables and responsibilities be discussed.

A defined engagement

Scope, responsibilities and deliverables should be clear.

Before work starts, both sides should understand what is included, what is outside scope, who will provide information and who can approve decisions. A written proposal helps avoid uncertainty later.

A proposal should cover

  • The challenge and objectives.
  • Activities, methods and deliverables.
  • People responsible and client inputs.
  • Timeline, milestones and review points.
  • Fees, expenses, confidentiality and payment terms.
  • How changes and completion will be handled.

Before engaging a consultant

Questions to consider

Do we need to have a complete brief?

No. An initial conversation can help clarify the issue. It is useful to bring any available context, existing data, constraints and the people who need to be involved.

Can the scope change as the work progresses?

Changes should be discussed and approved through the process agreed in the proposal. Any effect on deliverables, fees or timing should be recorded before the additional work proceeds.

Are results guaranteed?

No. A consulting engagement can agree deliverables and a method of review, but business outcomes depend on many factors, including decisions and actions taken by the client organisation.
