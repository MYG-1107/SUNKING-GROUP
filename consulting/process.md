## 1. Initial discovery

The first conversation identifies the organisation, decision-makers, current situation, constraints and the issue to be addressed. It is an opportunity to understand whether the enquiry matches the group's areas of work.

## 2. Scope and objectives

If the engagement is suitable, both sides define the objectives, work areas, stakeholders, assumptions, exclusions, timeline and responsibilities. Any data access or confidentiality requirements should be raised at this stage.

## 3. Proposal and agreement

A written proposal should set out the approach, deliverables, milestones, fees, expenses, payment schedule, intellectual-property arrangements, data handling, cancellation terms and change-control process. Work should begin only after the relevant parties approve the agreement.

## 4. Delivery and communication

The project proceeds against agreed milestones. The parties should establish how progress, risks, decisions and changes will be recorded and who is authorised to approve them.

## 5. Review and close-out

The final stage checks the deliverables against the agreed scope, captures lessons and confirms any remaining actions. Outcomes should be reported accurately and supported by evidence.

## No assumed results

A consulting engagement does not guarantee a particular financial, operational or organisational result. Outcomes depend on the agreed scope, the quality of available information and decisions made by the client organisation.
