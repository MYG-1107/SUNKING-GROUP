## Doctoral study: PhD and DBA

A **PhD** typically centres on original academic research that contributes to knowledge in a field. A **Doctor of Business Administration (DBA)** is generally a professional doctorate with an applied focus on business and organisational practice. The exact distinction, thesis requirements and award title are defined by the institution.

Published SUNKING GROUP materials also refer to doctoral and post-doctoral research support. The scope may include research planning and academic development; confirm the precise services, responsible academic personnel and boundaries of support before engaging.

## Who may be eligible?

Doctoral applicants are commonly expected to hold a relevant master’s degree or an equivalent qualification. Some universities may consider exceptional applicants with other qualifications and substantial experience. Additional requirements can include:

- A clear research area and preliminary research question.
- A research proposal aligned with the university’s faculty or research priorities.
- Academic transcripts and a CV.
- Evidence of research-methods preparation or relevant professional experience.
- References, language evidence and an interview.
- An ethics application where the proposed work involves participants or sensitive data.

Postdoctoral research is normally undertaken after completion of a doctoral degree. Fellowship, visiting researcher and postdoctoral appointment criteria vary substantially by institution and funding body.

## Duration and expected milestones

Doctoral completion timelines depend on the discipline, full-time or part-time registration, institutional regulations, research access and progress review. The existing DBA summary lists an indicative three-to-five-year period; this is not a promise of completion time and must be checked against the current university regulations. PhD and postdoctoral timelines must be obtained from the institution or funder.

## Research capabilities and outcomes

A well-designed research journey aims to develop the ability to:

- Formulate a focused and researchable question.
- Review literature and identify a defensible research gap.
- Select methods appropriate to the question and evidence.
- Collect, manage and analyse data ethically.
- Communicate findings through a thesis, presentation or publication.
- Explain limitations and the potential contribution of the work.

Publication, graduation, funding and career outcomes are not guaranteed.

## Enquiry steps

1. Describe your academic background, research topic and current stage.
2. Identify whether you are seeking programme information, proposal feedback, research-methods development or another defined service.
3. Ask which institution awards the qualification and who is responsible for admission and supervision.
4. Confirm scope, fees, timelines, confidentiality, authorship and research-integrity expectations in writing.
5. Apply to the university through its official doctoral or postdoctoral process.

All research work must follow the relevant institution’s academic-integrity, ethics, authorship and data-protection policies. Support must not replace the candidate’s own intellectual contribution.
