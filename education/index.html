How learning can work

Connect knowledge with practice.

Experiential learning gives learners an opportunity to work with situations, reflect on what happened, connect those observations with concepts and test what they have learned in a new context.

This approach is useful in management education because decisions are rarely made from theory alone. Professional judgement also develops through practice, feedback and reflection.

A simple learning cycle

  1. Experience: work through a case, task or real situation.
  2. Reflect: consider the decisions, evidence and results.
  3. Understand: connect the experience with concepts and frameworks.
  4. Apply: use the learning in a new situation and review the outcome.
Before you apply

Questions worth answering first

It is easier to compare programmes when the same questions are asked of each provider.

Who awards the qualification?

Identify the legal name of the awarding institution and check that it is responsible for the exact qualification being advertised. A business partner, agent or adviser is not automatically the awarding institution.

Will the qualification meet my career or further-study needs?

Check the rules of the regulator, employer, professional body or university where you intend to use the qualification. Recognition can depend on the country, institution, programme and intended use.

What is the total cost?

Ask for a dated fee schedule that includes tuition, registration, examinations, dissertation, graduation and any other required charges. Confirm the currency and refund terms.

What does the study format require?

For online or blended learning, confirm scheduled sessions, attendance expectations, time zones, assessment methods, technology requirements and any required campus visits.

Who supports me after admission?

Ask which organisation provides academic guidance, technical assistance, student records, formal complaints handling and accessibility support.

Not sure which route is right?

Tell us about your highest completed qualification, subject interests and the outcome you are working towards.
