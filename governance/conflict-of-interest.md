## What may constitute a conflict?

A conflict of interest can arise when a personal, financial, family, professional or organisational relationship could influence—or reasonably appear to influence—a person's judgement. A conflict does not automatically mean that misconduct has occurred; it means the situation should be disclosed and assessed.

Examples may include:

- A financial interest in a supplier or service provider being considered.
- A close personal relationship with an applicant, learner, employee or contractor.
- An external role that overlaps with a decision being made.
- A research or publication interest that could affect independent assessment.
- A referral or commission arrangement relevant to a recommendation.

## Disclosure and review

A person who identifies an actual, potential or perceived conflict should disclose it to the relevant decision-maker as early as possible. The review should consider the nature of the interest, the decision affected and proportionate safeguards.

Possible measures include documenting the disclosure, restricting access to information, separating duties, arranging independent review or withdrawing from a decision.

## Academic and partner relationships

Where an external university, journal, employer or client has its own conflict-of-interest rules, those rules must also be followed. A general disclosure to SUNKING GROUP does not replace a formal declaration required by another organisation.

## Ask for the applicable procedure

The formal reporting route and record-keeping requirements should be confirmed for the relevant programme, workplace or contract.
