## Who can participate?

This training can suit students preparing for professional environments, early-career employees, customer-facing staff, project teams, supervisors and managers. A general awareness session can be designed for mixed-experience groups; advanced workshops may benefit from participants bringing examples from their own work.

No particular qualification is normally required for a general skills workshop. The organisation and training team should agree any role-specific or language requirements before scheduling.

## Indicative duration

A focused session may run for **half a day or one day**. A broader workshop with practice, peer feedback and follow-up activities may be arranged over **two days** or as a multi-session series over **two to four weeks**. These are design options; actual contact hours and dates depend on the agreed scope.

## Topics and intended outcomes

Possible topics include clear written and verbal communication, active listening, non-verbal communication, giving and receiving feedback, emotional self-awareness, empathy, difficult conversations, negotiation and resolving workplace misunderstandings.

Participants can practise how to:

- Structure messages for a clear audience and purpose.
- Listen, ask clarifying questions and check understanding.
- Recognise emotional responses and choose a considered response.
- Give specific, respectful and actionable feedback.
- Prepare for difficult conversations and manage disagreement constructively.
- Adapt communication to colleagues, clients and stakeholders.

The programme can use short scenarios, role-play, reflection and workplace action planning. Outcomes should be reviewed against the objectives agreed at the start.

## Enquiry and registration steps

1. Identify the participant group, work setting and preferred language.
2. Describe the communication situations that need attention.
3. Select the intended outcomes and an appropriate session format.
4. Confirm the trainer, syllabus, learning materials, fees and delivery arrangements.
5. Register participants through the process confirmed in the proposal.
6. Collect feedback and agree any follow-up support included in the engagement.
