## Who is this training for?

The programme can be designed for first-time managers, team leaders, supervisors, department heads, experienced managers and professionals preparing for wider responsibility. There is generally no formal academic prerequisite for a customised workplace workshop, although the organisation may define its own participant criteria.

A short needs discussion helps identify the participants’ roles, experience, organisational context and current leadership challenges. This allows examples and activities to be pitched at an appropriate level.

## Duration and delivery options

Possible formats include:

- **Half-day or one-day session:** introductions to a focused leadership topic.
- **Two-day workshop:** case work, role-play, peer discussion and action planning.
- **Two-to-six-week learning series:** several sessions with workplace application between sessions.
- **Longer development pathway:** modular learning, coaching or follow-up activities where separately agreed.

Delivery may be in person, virtual or blended, subject to trainer and participant availability. These are planning formats, not guaranteed dates or a standard off-the-shelf timetable.

## Topics and learning outcomes

Topics can include situational and transformational leadership, emotional intelligence, strategic thinking, ethical decision-making, communication, delegation, coaching, influencing and leading change.

By the end of an agreed programme, participants should be better prepared to:

- Reflect on their leadership approach and its effect on others.
- Set direction and communicate priorities clearly.
- Make decisions with awareness of evidence, risk and trade-offs.
- Delegate work and support accountability.
- Hold constructive feedback and coaching conversations.
- Translate learning into an individual action plan.

Outcomes depend on the agreed syllabus, participant engagement and opportunities to apply learning at work. Training does not guarantee promotion or performance improvement.

## How to request a programme

1. Share the organisation, participant roles and approximate group size.
2. Describe the leadership needs and situations the team wants to address.
3. Agree learning objectives, topics, duration, delivery mode and assessment approach.
4. Review the proposal, trainer information, fee and cancellation terms.
5. Confirm the schedule and participant registration process in writing.
6. Review feedback and follow-up actions after delivery, where included in the agreement.
