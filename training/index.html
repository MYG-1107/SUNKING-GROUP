Start with the challenge, not a catalogue of topics

A team may need clearer communication. A new manager may need support with delegation and feedback. An organisation going through change may need its leaders to communicate decisions and help people adapt. These are different situations and should not be treated as the same training requirement.

SUNKING GROUP's training work focuses on management and leadership capabilities that can be practised and applied. Before suggesting a format, we discuss the audience, current context, skills to develop and what participants should be able to do differently after the programme.

How a programme is planned

Clear objectives and useful practice.

Training is most useful when participants can connect the session to the work they actually do. The programme plan should explain its purpose, learning activities and how the organisation will review its value.

Methods may include

  • Relevant workplace scenarios and case discussions.
  • Role-play and structured practice of conversations.
  • Group problem-solving and reflection.
  • Action planning for the weeks after the session.
  • Feedback and agreed learning checks.
From enquiry to delivery

What to expect when planning training

Before any session is confirmed, both sides should agree who is attending, what will be covered and how delivery will work.

Understand the need

Discuss the business context, participant roles and the capability gap or change to address.

Agree the programme

Define learning objectives, content, format, group size, trainer responsibilities and materials.

Confirm delivery

Agree date, venue or platform, language, accessibility needs, fees and cancellation terms in writing.

Review learning

Gather participant feedback and review the learning checks or actions agreed for the workplace.

Practical planning

Questions organisations should consider

Who should attend?

Identify the participant group and whether the programme is for new managers, experienced leaders, project teams, administrative staff or a mixed audience.

How much time should we set aside?

Duration depends on the objectives and amount of practice required. A short workshop may introduce a topic; developing and applying new habits can require follow-up sessions or workplace activity.

Can the training be delivered online or on site?

Possible formats can be discussed during scoping. The agreed proposal should specify the delivery mode, location, technology, schedule and any participant requirements.

How do we know whether it helped?

Agree what will be reviewed before the programme starts. This may include participant feedback, knowledge checks, practice activities or follow-up actions. Do not assume a workshop alone guarantees a measurable business result.
