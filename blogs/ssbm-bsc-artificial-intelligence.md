## Why consider an undergraduate degree in artificial intelligence?

Artificial intelligence is influencing areas such as business operations, healthcare, finance, manufacturing, education, logistics and media. An undergraduate programme in AI can provide a foundation for understanding how data-driven systems are designed, evaluated and applied. Students should look for a balanced curriculum that develops technical understanding alongside mathematics, problem-solving, communication and responsible technology practice.

SSBM Geneva publishes a **Bachelor of Science (BSc) in Artificial Intelligence**. Its programme page describes a campus-based undergraduate degree and identifies themes including artificial intelligence and data science, generative AI and emerging technologies, and AI applications in business and society. Review the live [SSBM BSc in Artificial Intelligence page](https://www.ssbm.ch/bachelor-of-science-bsc-in-artificial-intelligence/) for the current curriculum, duration, entry criteria and fees.

## What should an AI bachelor's curriculum include?

The phrase "artificial intelligence" can cover a wide range of subjects. Applicants should examine the detailed module list to understand the balance between computing fundamentals, data handling and applied use cases.

A strong technical foundation may include programming, mathematics, statistics, data structures, machine learning concepts and methods for evaluating model performance. Applied learning may address natural language processing, computer vision, generative AI, automation or data-driven decision-making. Responsible AI topics should address fairness, privacy, security, reliability and the consequences of deploying automated systems.

Do not assume all these topics are included simply because a course is called an AI degree. Ask for the current curriculum and module descriptions.

## The connection between AI and business

AI systems are developed and used within organisations, so technology graduates benefit from understanding the context in which systems operate. A predictive model, for example, may support demand planning, but its value depends on data quality, operational integration and the cost of acting on its output. A generative AI tool may improve a workflow, but organisations still need to assess accuracy, copyright, confidentiality and human oversight.

The programme's published emphasis on AI applications in business and society makes it important to ask how students develop the ability to connect technical concepts with practical needs. Look for projects where students define a problem, work with data, evaluate results and explain limitations.

## Is an AI bachelor's degree right for you?

You may want to investigate this route if you enjoy mathematical reasoning, structured problem-solving, programming and technology. You should be prepared to practise regularly and work through complex concepts. AI is not only about using chatbots or writing prompts; technical study requires a deeper understanding of data, algorithms, systems and evaluation.

If your main interest is management rather than building or evaluating AI systems, an MBA or management programme with AI-related subjects may be a better fit. Compare the intended learning outcomes rather than assuming that any course containing "AI" will provide the same skills.

## Campus study in Geneva

The provider currently describes the BSc in Artificial Intelligence as an on-campus programme in Geneva. Confirm the teaching calendar, exact campus location, attendance obligations, language requirements and student support. International applicants should budget for tuition and living costs and verify visa and residence requirements with the appropriate Swiss authorities.

An academic offer does not in itself confer permission to enter, reside or work in Switzerland. Seek official immigration guidance for your nationality and circumstances before making commitments.

## Admissions and cost checks

Review the accepted school-leaving qualifications and any subject prerequisites. Ask whether mathematics or computing preparation is recommended or required. Confirm how international certificates are assessed, whether English proficiency evidence is needed and which records must be submitted.

Use the current [SSBM tuition-fee schedule](https://www.ssbm.ch/tuition-fees/) and request a written budget that covers tuition, any additional academic charges, travel and living expenses. Do not depend on an old web advertisement for the final cost.

## Questions to ask before applying

- What programming languages and mathematical topics are taught?
- How much of the curriculum is practical project work?
- Are students required to complete a capstone or final project?
- What hardware, software and computing resources are provided?
- How does the programme teach responsible AI and data privacy?
- What are the entry requirements and accepted secondary qualifications?
- What is the current timetable and campus attendance expectation?
- Which support services are available to students?

The answers help you understand whether the course matches your interests and readiness.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the SSBM BSc in Artificial Intelligence online?

The official programme page currently lists a campus format. Confirm the format and attendance conditions for the intake you are considering.

### Is AI the same as data science?

The subjects overlap but are not identical. AI focuses on systems that perform tasks associated with intelligent behaviour, while data science covers methods for extracting insight from data. A programme may include both.

### Do I need prior programming experience?

Entry requirements and recommended preparation should be checked with SSBM. Ask whether beginners are supported and whether specific mathematics or computing subjects are expected.

### Does the degree guarantee an AI job?

No qualification guarantees a job. Employers consider technical skill, practical work, communication, experience and local employment conditions.
