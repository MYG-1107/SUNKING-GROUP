## What is a Doctor of Education?

The Doctor of Education (Ed.D.) is a professional doctorate for people working in education, learning, administration and related fields. It typically focuses on applying research to real-world educational challenges. The exact academic structure and award requirements depend on the institution.

SSBM Geneva publishes an **Online Doctor of Education (Ed.D.)**. Its public programme description identifies three broad themes: educational leadership and transformation, digital innovation and future learning, and research-driven educational impact. It also refers to topics such as AI in education, digital learning environments, curriculum innovation, institutional strategy and evidence-informed decision-making. Review the current [SSBM Ed.D. programme page](https://www.ssbm.ch/doctor-of-education-ed-d/) for the live curriculum and admission rules.

## Who might consider an Ed.D.?

The programme may interest school leaders, university administrators, academic managers, corporate learning professionals, education consultants and experienced educators who want to investigate an issue in educational practice.

The key question is whether you have a meaningful problem to research. Examples might include improving learner engagement in an online programme, evaluating the adoption of educational technology, developing institutional approaches to quality assurance or understanding the effect of a leadership intervention. These are illustrative themes, not guaranteed specialisations.

## Education leadership and organisational change

Educational institutions face challenges that involve curriculum, technology, finance, staff development, student support, governance and public expectations. Leaders must make decisions while considering academic standards, learner needs, staff capability and institutional resources.

Doctoral study can provide a structured way to investigate one of these issues. A research project should define its context, draw on relevant literature and collect evidence using suitable methods. The findings should be interpreted carefully and translated into recommendations that are supported by the evidence.

## Digital innovation and future learning

Digital platforms and AI tools are changing how educational content is created, delivered and assessed. They may enable new forms of access and personalisation, but they also raise concerns about privacy, bias, academic integrity, accuracy and the quality of human interaction.

A useful research project should move beyond broad claims that technology will transform education. It should identify a defined learning or organisational problem and examine whether a particular approach improves outcomes under specific conditions. It should also consider who may be disadvantaged and how risks can be managed.

## Research expectations

An Ed.D. is a doctoral programme and should be approached as sustained academic work. Applicants should understand the research methodology requirements, proposal development, supervision arrangements and final thesis or dissertation assessment. Ask how the programme supports participants who are studying while employed.

A suitable proposal explains the problem, research question, relevant literature, methods, expected contribution and ethical considerations. Educational research often involves students, teachers, staff or institutional records, so consent, confidentiality and safeguarding need careful attention.

## Entry requirements and documents

SSBM Geneva's published doctoral admission information describes entry routes involving an MBA or equivalent, or a relevant master's degree with professional experience, alongside academic records and application documents. The specific Ed.D. requirements may differ from other doctorates. Confirm the exact criteria with the admissions team.

Prepare transcripts, certificates, CV, identity documentation and the research information requested. If your background is in education but your master's degree has a different title, ask how the institution assesses its relevance. Do not assume that years of teaching alone replace academic entry requirements.

## Online format and workload

SSBM lists its Ed.D. programme as online. Confirm the current combination of recorded content, live seminars, individual meetings, assignments and research milestones. Ask how often you will meet a mentor or supervisor and how progress is evaluated.

Online delivery can help working professionals manage location constraints, but it does not remove the need for regular research time. Candidates should plan for reading, proposal writing, data collection, analysis and thesis drafting. If the research depends on your workplace, secure permission and data access early.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an Ed.D. the same as a PhD in Education?

No. Both can be doctoral qualifications, but their design and research purpose may differ. Compare the exact regulations, thesis requirements and career expectations.

### Is the SSBM Ed.D. online?

The official programme page currently lists an online format. Confirm the latest study arrangements and any live-session requirements.

### Can an education administrator apply?

The programme description is aimed at educators, administrators, executives and learning professionals. Formal entry requirements still apply and must be confirmed by SSBM.

### Does an Ed.D. guarantee a senior education role?

No. A doctoral qualification does not guarantee promotion or employment. Experience, institutional requirements and local employment conditions also matter.
