## What is a Master of Science in Management?

A Master of Science in Management is a postgraduate qualification focused on management knowledge and the analysis of organisations. It may suit graduates who want to deepen their understanding of leadership, strategy, finance, operations and business environments. The exact academic emphasis depends on the programme's curriculum, assessment structure and entry criteria.

SSBM Geneva publishes a **Master of Science (MSc) in Management** with online and Geneva campus programme information. The provider's official pages describe themes such as global context, leadership and management, and topics including global management, strategic leadership, project management, financial management, human resources, marketing, data analytics, sales, digital transformation and operations. Check the [online MSc page](https://www.ssbm.ch/master-of-science-msc-in-management/) or the [Geneva MSc page](https://www.ssbm.ch/master-of-science-msc-in-management-geneva/) for the current format and curriculum.

## What can you learn in management study?

Management is an applied discipline. Students may need to understand how organisations set priorities, allocate resources, coordinate people and respond to changing markets. A management master's can provide a structured way to examine how different business functions interact.

Strategy and leadership modules may explore how organisations make long-term decisions and manage change. Finance helps learners understand investment, performance and resource allocation. Marketing connects customer needs with product, pricing and communication choices. Operations and project management examine how plans are translated into results. Analytics helps managers interpret information while recognising the limitations of data.

The curriculum should explain how these areas are taught and assessed. Ask whether the programme includes applied projects, a research component or a final thesis.

## MSc Management or MBA: which is the better fit?

An MSc in Management and an MBA can both develop business knowledge, but they are not interchangeable. A management master's may place greater emphasis on academic frameworks and specialist management analysis. An MBA often targets broad management development and can be designed for professionals with work experience. The actual distinction depends on the provider and the programme.

Compare the entry criteria and intended audience. Check whether experience is required, how much research is included, whether the course is designed for recent graduates or established professionals, and what the qualification enables you to study next. Read our [MBA after a bachelor's degree guide](/blogs/ssbm-mba-after-bachelors/) for more context.

## Online or campus format

SSBM's published information includes management master's routes in different formats. Do not assume that the programme page for one delivery mode describes the other. Compare the learning platform, timetable, faculty interaction, assessment, campus attendance and available student services.

For online study, check whether sessions are recorded or live and what deadlines apply. For campus study in Geneva, ask about attendance, calendar, accommodation and travel. A format that looks convenient on paper may not fit your actual responsibilities.

## Entry requirements and application documents

SSBM's admissions information describes an undergraduate degree from a recognised university or equivalent as a common entry requirement for master's-level study, alongside transcripts and application records. Programme-specific criteria can vary. Confirm how your prior degree, academic results and language evidence will be assessed.

Prepare certificates, transcripts, CV, identity documentation and any additional items requested by the school. Keep copies of your application and ask for clarification if your qualification title or grading system differs from the examples in the admissions guidance.

## Cost and practical planning

Review total tuition, payment dates, currency, additional charges and cancellation terms. If you are comparing online and campus options, also consider travel or living costs. Ask whether course materials, examinations, graduation activities or other services involve separate charges.

SSBM publishes its own [tuition-fee information](https://www.ssbm.ch/tuition-fees/). The official current schedule and a written fee statement should be used for budgeting rather than a third-party summary.

## Questions to ask before enrolling

- Which exact MSc award is granted and by whom?
- What are the programme's learning outcomes?
- Which subjects are compulsory?
- Is there a thesis, research project or capstone?
- What is the study mode and weekly workload?
- What are the academic entry and language requirements?
- How are assignments and examinations assessed?
- What are the full fees and refund conditions?

The answers are more useful than a general ranking or a short promotional description.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the SSBM MSc in Management available online?

SSBM has an online programme page and a separate Geneva campus page. Confirm current availability and delivery details on the page for the exact route you intend to apply for.

### Is an MSc in Management the same as an MBA?

No. They are different qualification types with potentially different audiences, structures and admissions requirements.

### Can I apply with a non-business bachelor's degree?

That depends on the programme and how SSBM assesses your qualification. Ask the admissions team for a formal clarification.

### Does the programme guarantee a management job?

No. A qualification can develop knowledge, but employment depends on experience, skills, market conditions and employer requirements.
