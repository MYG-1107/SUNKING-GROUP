## Two doctoral routes with different purposes

A Doctor of Business Administration (DBA) and a Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) are both doctoral-level qualifications, but they are not the same award and should not be chosen based only on the word "doctor". Their research purposes, programme structures and expectations can differ between institutions and countries.

A DBA is commonly framed as a professional doctorate with an emphasis on applying research to complex business or organisational practice. A PhD generally focuses on making an original contribution to academic knowledge within a discipline. These are broad distinctions, not universal rules; the specific programme regulations determine the actual structure and award.

## What is a DBA?

A DBA is often designed for experienced professionals who want to investigate a significant management or business question using rigorous research. The topic may come from an organisational challenge such as digital transformation, leadership, strategy, supply-chain resilience, innovation, governance or performance improvement.

The quality of a DBA depends on more than workplace relevance. A doctoral project must define a clear problem, engage with relevant literature, use a defensible research design, analyse evidence carefully and make a contribution that meets the institution's academic standards. Research findings should not be treated as consulting opinions or personal experience alone.

SSBM's published DBA pages describe professional doctoral study with coursework or research modules and a thesis or dissertation component, depending on the route. Read the exact programme specification to understand supervision, research milestones, assessment and expected outputs.

## What is a PhD?

A PhD is usually a research-intensive academic doctorate. Candidates formulate research questions, review the scholarly literature, select appropriate methods, collect or analyse evidence and produce a thesis that contributes new knowledge. The subject area and research expectations are defined by the university and discipline.

A PhD may be appropriate for candidates who want to pursue academic research, teaching or specialist research roles. It can also be relevant outside universities, particularly where employers value advanced research capability. As with a DBA, no doctoral qualification guarantees a particular job or academic appointment.

## Compare the research question

The label "applied" does not mean a DBA uses less rigorous research. A practice-oriented research question still needs a sound theoretical basis and appropriate methods. For example, a DBA candidate might investigate how a particular change-management approach affects adoption of a technology in a defined organisational setting. A PhD candidate might investigate a broader theoretical question about organisational change or technology adoption within a discipline.

Both projects require clear scope. A question that is too broad can become unmanageable; a question that is too narrow may not support a meaningful contribution. The candidate should understand the available data, ethical requirements, access permissions and the expertise needed to complete the research.

## Who might consider a DBA?

A DBA may be worth investigating if you have substantial professional experience and want to examine a problem connected to management practice. Potential applicants may include senior managers, consultants, entrepreneurs, public-sector leaders or specialists responsible for complex organisational decisions.

Applicants should be ready for sustained reading, academic writing, research design and independent work. Professional experience can help identify practical questions, but it does not replace the academic preparation required for doctoral research.

## Who might consider a PhD?

A PhD may fit someone whose main goal is to pursue an academic research question and contribute to a scholarly field. Applicants should examine the university's research groups, supervisor expertise, funding structure, methodological expectations and publication culture. The availability of a suitable supervisor can be critical.

## Questions to ask before enrolling

- What is the exact award title and awarding institution?
- Is the qualification a professional doctorate or a PhD?
- What prior degree and work experience are required?
- What research methods are taught or expected?
- How is supervision assigned and delivered?
- What are the thesis or dissertation requirements?
- Are ethics approval and organisational permissions required?
- How is the final research examined?
- What are the full costs and completion rules?

For professional doctorates, ask how the programme balances taught modules with independent research. For a PhD, understand the expected original contribution and supervision model.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a DBA equal to a PhD?

They are different doctoral qualifications. Their standing and recognition depend on the awarding institution, applicable national frameworks and the rules of the organisation or regulator evaluating the award.

### Is a DBA easier than a PhD?

It should not be assumed to be easier. A DBA requires doctoral-level research and academic standards, even where the research is connected to professional practice.

### Can a DBA lead to academic work?

Some DBA graduates pursue teaching or research activities, but requirements differ by institution and role. Check the expectations of the universities or employers where you intend to work.
