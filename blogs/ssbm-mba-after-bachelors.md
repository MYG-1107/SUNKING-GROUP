## The short answer depends on the programme

A bachelor's degree is a common academic starting point for MBA admission, but the exact requirements depend on the institution and the programme. Some MBA routes are designed for graduates who are early in their careers; executive routes may expect additional management experience or further postgraduate education. The word "MBA" does not describe one universal admissions standard.

SSBM Geneva's published [admissions overview](https://www.ssbm.ch/admissions-overview/) identifies an undergraduate degree from a recognised university or equivalent as a common requirement for MBA programmes. It also lists transcripts, a CV or résumé, identity documentation, a photograph and English-language evidence where applicable. The school may apply additional requirements to particular courses.

## Which bachelor's degrees may be relevant?

Applicants often come from business administration, commerce, engineering, computer science, economics, science, healthcare, law and other disciplines. A business degree can provide prior exposure to accounting, marketing or management. A technical or professional degree may provide deeper subject expertise but less formal study of business functions.

The important question is not whether your degree title contains the word "business". It is whether your qualification meets the institution's entry requirements and whether you can explain why an MBA is the right next step. SSBM Geneva assesses applications against its published criteria, so confirm whether your degree, awarding institution and academic record are acceptable.

## Does work experience matter?

Work experience can help an applicant connect management theory with workplace decisions. It may also be a formal entry requirement for certain executive routes. The amount and type of experience expected can differ, so do not assume that all MBA programmes require identical professional histories.

If you have worked in a technical role, consider how your responsibilities have involved projects, budgets, customers, teams, suppliers or business outcomes. If you are a recent graduate, think about whether a broad MBA is timely or whether you would benefit from gaining professional experience first. A qualification should fit your current stage rather than serve as a shortcut to a job title.

## Choosing between an MBA and a specialist master's

An MBA generally provides broad management education across functions. A specialist master's may focus more deeply on a discipline or academic area. For example, someone seeking advanced study in management theory may compare an MSc in Management, while a professional who wants wider general management learning may explore an MBA. The curriculum and academic level should guide the decision, not assumptions about which title is more prestigious.

SSBM Geneva publishes a [Master of Science in Management](https://www.ssbm.ch/master-of-science-msc-in-management/) as well as MBA programmes. Compare the exact modules, entry requirements, assessment methods and delivery formats.

## A simple decision checklist

Before applying, answer these questions:

1. What is my highest completed qualification?
2. Is my bachelor's degree awarded by a recognised institution?
3. Can I provide the final certificate and full transcript?
4. Does the selected programme require professional experience?
5. Do I meet any English-language requirement?
6. Do I want broad management development or a specialist subject?
7. Can I manage the workload alongside employment or other commitments?
8. Have I checked the full fees and withdrawal terms?

If your qualification is from another country, ask whether a credential evaluation or additional evidence is needed. Provide the exact award title and institution name rather than relying on a translated title alone.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a fresh graduate apply for an MBA?

A bachelor's degree may satisfy a common academic entry condition, but the programme may have additional expectations. Contact SSBM Geneva to confirm whether your experience level fits the specific MBA.

### Can I study an MBA after engineering?

Applicants from different academic fields may consider management study. The institution will assess the actual qualification and any additional programme requirements.

### Is an MBA better than an MSc in Management?

Neither is universally better. An MBA usually focuses on broad business and managerial practice, while an MSc may have a different academic structure and subject emphasis. Compare programme specifications.

### Should I work before applying?

That depends on the programme, your goals and your readiness for postgraduate study. Work experience may strengthen your application and help you connect learning with professional practice, but check the course's formal requirements.
