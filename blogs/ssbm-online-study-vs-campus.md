## Choose the learning format as carefully as the course title

When comparing business degrees, applicants often focus on the qualification and subject area first. The study format can be just as important. An online course, a campus programme in Geneva and a blended route can lead to very different weekly routines, travel requirements and learning experiences.

SSBM Geneva's programme finder lists several formats, including online, face-to-face, live virtual and blended options. Not every programme is available in every format. Use the [official programme finder](https://www.ssbm.ch/course-search/) to check the format attached to the specific course rather than assuming it from the qualification category.

## Online study: flexibility and self-management

Online study can reduce the need to relocate or travel to campus. It may be helpful for professionals who need to combine learning with work or family responsibilities. The actual level of flexibility depends on how the programme is organised.

Ask whether learning is self-paced, whether live sessions are compulsory, whether there are scheduled assessments and how group work is organised. A programme can be online and still have strict deadlines. Students need to manage their time, keep up with reading and participate consistently.

Consider your working environment. Reliable internet access, a suitable study space and the ability to concentrate independently are important. If you often work across time zones, check the timing of live sessions.

## Campus study in Geneva: direct interaction and attendance

Campus education provides scheduled in-person contact and access to the physical learning environment. It may suit students who value classroom discussion, structured timetables and face-to-face interaction. It also requires careful planning.

Before choosing a Geneva campus programme, confirm the exact attendance calendar, location, class schedule, examination arrangements and any compulsory activities. Budget for travel, accommodation, meals and other living costs. If you are an international applicant, check visa and residence conditions with official authorities. Admission to an academic programme should never be treated as immigration permission.

## Blended study: combining two modes

A blended programme combines online learning with in-person elements. This may provide flexibility while retaining some campus contact. The ratio and design can vary, so read the exact programme specification.

Ask what happens online and what happens on campus. Are campus sessions used for workshops, presentations or examinations? Are they concentrated into a few blocks or distributed throughout the year? How are online and face-to-face activities assessed? The answers determine whether a blended course is practical for your location and schedule.

## Compare the real workload

A flexible format does not remove the workload. Estimate the time required for learning activities, reading, assignments, group work and revision. Ask the institution for an indicative weekly commitment and academic calendar. Compare this with your current work pattern.

If your job includes frequent travel, a programme with fixed live sessions may be difficult. If you need regular interaction to stay motivated, a fully independent format may require additional discipline. The right choice is the one you can complete with consistent engagement.

## Compare total cost, not tuition alone

Online and campus tuition may differ, but the overall financial comparison should include more than the published tuition price. For campus study, consider accommodation, transport, meals, travel and insurance. For online study, consider equipment, connectivity, possible software and any optional in-person activities. Confirm all additional charges with the institution.

Review SSBM's current [tuition-fee information](https://www.ssbm.ch/tuition-fees/) and request a written fee schedule. Check payment dates, instalments, currency, deferral terms and refund conditions before paying.

## A simple decision matrix

| Your priority | What to investigate |
| --- | --- |
| Continue full-time work | Online schedule, workload and live-session requirements |
| Face-to-face classroom contact | Campus timetable and attendance expectations |
| Reduce international travel | Online availability and any compulsory in-person activity |
| Mix classroom and remote learning | Blended ratio and campus session calendar |
| Study with a group | Group projects, live discussion and cohort structure |
| Manage a limited budget | Total fees plus travel, living and equipment costs |

Use this matrix as a starting point, then compare programme-specific details.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an online degree easier than a campus degree?

Not necessarily. The mode changes how learning is delivered, not the academic standard or the work required to complete the programme.

### Are all SSBM programmes online?

No. The SSBM catalogue includes multiple formats, and availability varies by programme. Check the current finder.

### Can a blended programme be completed from another country?

That depends on the campus attendance rules and any travel or immigration requirements. Confirm the schedule with SSBM before applying.

### Does campus study guarantee a Swiss visa?

No. Admission and immigration permission are separate decisions. Check official Swiss government guidance.
