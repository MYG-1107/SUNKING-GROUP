## What is a postdoctoral research fellowship?

A postdoctoral fellowship is a research-development opportunity for people who have already completed a doctoral qualification or meet the provider's stated equivalent criteria. It is not a first doctorate, a master's degree or a general short course. The focus is usually on extending a research profile, developing new work and contributing to scholarly or professional knowledge.

SSBM Geneva publishes a **Postdoctoral Research Fellowship Program**. Its public information describes a hybrid format and research themes across areas such as business, management, technology, sustainability and innovation. The page also refers to mentorship, research methods, academic publishing and engagement with a multidisciplinary research centre. Review the current [SSBM postdoctoral fellowship page](https://www.ssbm.ch/postdoctoral-research-fellowship-program/) for the exact eligibility, deliverables, duration, fee and application process.

## Who may be interested?

A postdoctoral route may interest PhD or DBA graduates who want to continue research after completing their doctorate. It may also be relevant to experienced professionals with doctoral qualifications who want to connect academic inquiry with industry challenges.

Applicants should be able to explain their previous research, the question they want to investigate next and how the proposed topic relates to the fellowship's focus. A doctorate alone does not guarantee acceptance. The provider may consider research fit, prior work, proposal quality, availability of supervision and other programme criteria.

## What research work might involve

Postdoctoral work can include developing a research agenda, reviewing new literature, refining methods, analysing data, drafting manuscripts, presenting findings and collaborating with other researchers. The exact output depends on the fellowship regulations. SSBM's published page refers to potential research outputs such as a peer-reviewed paper, white paper or industry case study, but applicants should confirm the current required deliverables.

Publication should never be treated as guaranteed. Peer-reviewed journals make independent editorial decisions, and research quality, relevance, methods and fit affect whether a manuscript is accepted. Applicants should ask what support the programme provides and what responsibilities remain with the fellow.

## Choosing a research topic

A useful postdoctoral proposal builds on the applicant's doctoral work without merely repeating it. It may extend a prior finding to another context, apply a new method, investigate a developing issue or connect academic research with an organisational challenge.

Potential areas may include digital transformation, responsible artificial intelligence, sustainable business, innovation management, leadership, organisational resilience or technology adoption. These are broad fields; a proposal should define a specific question, demonstrate why it matters and explain how the research can be conducted.

## Evidence to prepare

Applicants should organise their doctoral certificate, thesis or dissertation details, academic CV, publication list and a concise research proposal. The proposal should explain the problem, research question, relevant literature, methods, expected contribution and ethical considerations. Include evidence of prior research and explain any gaps or limitations in your publication record accurately.

If your doctoral qualification is from another country, check whether any additional documentation or recognition evidence is needed. If the research requires access to participants, company data or sensitive records, explain how permissions and confidentiality will be handled.

## Mentorship and research environment

Research support is a central point to clarify before joining a fellowship. Ask who provides supervision, how often progress meetings occur, what research resources are available and how feedback is documented. Confirm whether there are seminars, workshops, co-publication opportunities or access to research databases.

Clarify what "hybrid" means in practice. Ask which activities are online, which take place in person and whether any Geneva attendance is required. The label alone does not explain the timetable or travel commitment.

## Fees, outputs and intellectual property

Review the total fee and what it includes. Ask whether supervision, workshops, database access, events, publication support and any campus immersion are covered. Clarify whether any travel or conference costs are separate. Read the rules concerning authorship, intellectual property, publication approval, research data and the use of institutional affiliation.

A fellowship should not be selected based only on a promise of publication or a broad description of networking. Request written programme terms and understand exactly what completion means.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a PhD to apply?

SSBM's published fellowship information identifies doctoral qualifications such as a PhD or DBA as an expected background. Confirm the precise accepted qualifications and any equivalent routes with the provider.

### Is a postdoctoral fellowship another degree?

A fellowship is generally a research-development appointment or programme, not automatically a new academic degree. Review the formal completion documentation and award terminology.

### Does the programme guarantee a journal publication?

No responsible applicant should assume that a journal will accept a paper. Confirm the programme's required deliverables and understand that journal editors and reviewers make independent decisions.

### Is the SSBM fellowship fully online?

The published programme description identifies a hybrid format. Confirm which activities require in-person participation.

## Plan your application

Prepare a research statement that is specific, feasible and aligned with the provider's published focus. Ask for the current application requirements and fee schedule. Compare the fellowship with the research expectations of your current institution or other potential hosts.
