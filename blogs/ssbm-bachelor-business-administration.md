## What does a Bachelor in Business Administration prepare you to study?

A Bachelor in Business Administration provides an undergraduate foundation in how organisations operate. The subject usually connects several business areas: management, marketing, finance, accounting, human resources, operations, entrepreneurship and strategy. The aim is to understand how decisions in one part of an organisation affect the others.

SSBM Geneva publishes a Geneva Bachelor in Business Administration (Swiss) programme. Its programme page describes a campus-based undergraduate route in Geneva. The school's [Geneva bachelor programme information](https://www.ssbm.ch/geneva-bachelor-programs/) should be checked for the current qualification title, curriculum, entry criteria, format and fee schedule.

## Why choose business administration?

Business administration can be relevant to students who want a broad foundation before selecting a sector or function. A graduate may later work in operations, business development, marketing, finance support, project coordination, entrepreneurship or further study. The degree itself does not guarantee a particular job; employers consider skills, experience, communication, location, language and market conditions.

A broad business curriculum is especially useful for learners who are still exploring where their interests lie. It can help them understand the connections between customer needs, cash flow, people, processes and strategy. Students should also build practical capabilities outside formal modules, such as writing, presenting, teamwork, data literacy and responsible technology use.

## What should you expect from the curriculum?

Review the actual course list rather than relying on a generic description of business education. Look for a sensible progression from introductory business concepts to more advanced analysis. Ask how the programme covers quantitative skills, financial reasoning, organisational behaviour, marketing, operations and business ethics.

Consider how learning is assessed. Written assignments, group work, presentations, examinations, projects and internships each develop different capabilities. A programme may use case studies or practical projects, but you should confirm the exact format with the institution.

## Studying in Geneva

A campus-based programme creates a different experience from online study. Applicants should examine the campus location, timetable, attendance requirements, term dates and student-support arrangements. If you are an international applicant, consider travel, accommodation, insurance, living costs and the relevant immigration process.

An offer of admission is not the same as a residence permit or work authorisation. Do not make travel or employment plans until you have checked the official requirements with Swiss authorities and received appropriate guidance for your circumstances.

## Entry requirements and preparation

Bachelor's programmes commonly require completion of an accepted upper-secondary qualification or equivalent. SSBM Geneva's admissions pages describe the qualifications it accepts and any additional evidence required. International applicants should verify how their school-leaving certificate is assessed and whether translations or other records are needed.

Prepare a clear record of your education, subject results and identity documents. Ask about English-language requirements, application deadlines and any interview or additional assessment. If your documents use a different grading scale, provide the original record and follow the school's instructions for interpretation.

## How to compare the overall cost

Tuition is only one part of the budget for campus study. Consider accommodation, transport, meals, learning materials, health cover, travel, visa-related costs and personal expenses. Confirm the currency and payment schedule, and ask what happens if you defer or withdraw.

Use SSBM Geneva's current [tuition-fee information](https://www.ssbm.ch/tuition-fees/) as the starting point, then request a written estimate for the selected programme and intake. Do not rely on old promotional prices or assume that all fees are included in a headline figure.

## Questions for the admissions team

- What exact qualification is awarded?
- Which school-leaving certificates are accepted?
- What is the language of instruction?
- What are the teaching dates and attendance expectations?
- Which subjects are compulsory in the first year?
- Are internships, projects or practical activities part of the curriculum?
- What student services are available?
- What is the total cost of tuition and living in Geneva?
- What are the current visa and residence requirements for my nationality?

These questions help you compare an international study opportunity with other options in your home country.
